# Cross-resource reproducibility and incremental validity of colorectal cancer dependency maps

Cross-resource agreement in cancer dependency maps need not imply added information beyond existing models. We evaluated reproducibility, historical clinical precedence and pharmacologic association using independent three-dimensional (3D) resources and a lineage-matched two-dimensional (2D) comparator. Aggregate profiles from 18 Broad colorectal 3D screens, 67 Broad colorectal 2D screens and 81 Sanger colorectal models without annotated Broad overlap were compared across 15,008 genes. The strongest dependency decile contained 1,501 genes per 3D resource, with 763 shared genes (odds ratio [OR], 17.89); 645/763 (84.53%) were also highly ranked in 2D. Sanger concordance was 0.330 with Broad 3D and 0.353 with Broad 2D (Spearman difference, −0.0232; conditional gene-bootstrap 95% interval, −0.0330 to −0.0131). Among 994 selected strong-2D genes, all-three-profile membership was associated with historical Phase III-or-higher precedence after adjustment for 2D dependency strength and essentiality (OR, 4.22; 95% confidence interval, 1.19–14.97; nominal P=0.0259), but not Phase II-or-higher precedence (OR, 1.35; 0.59–3.11; P=0.482). The separate 3D-specific class showed no significant enrichment. Across 26 mapped inhibitory drugs, Broad 3D dependency correlated with independent metastatic-colorectal organoid drug sensitivity (ρ=0.420; P=0.0326), but its correlation advantage over 2D was uncertain (difference, 0.125; drug-bootstrap 95% interval, −0.086 to 0.362). These retrospective, aggregate comparisons identify reproducible dependencies and a selective historical-precedence association without establishing a generic 3D-specific advantage. Resource agreement, incremental information, clinical development history and pharmacologic association should be interpreted separately.
